METHODOLOGY & ASSUMPTIONS — WHAT'S MODELED FROM REAL PHYSICS VS. ILLUSTRATIVE
Grounded in real-world figures:
• Turbofan fuel flow (140 + throttle×2.2 + throttle²×0.02 lb/hr/engine) converted through actual Jet-A density (6.71 lb/US gal) against the 750-gal tank — same formula drives the Engine Health display, tank depletion, and route-range planning, so none of them can disagree.
• The quadratic term creates a genuine nm/gal efficiency peak at ~59% throttle, matching the real aerodynamic principle that fuel efficiency doesn't monotonically improve with power — it was tuned to produce that peak, not derived from actual wind-tunnel or CFD data for this airframe.
• APU and accessory-generator fuel/power figures (250kW APU ≈ 117 lb/hr, scaled linearly from a 60kW/28 lb/hr baseline; 50kW per engine accessory generator) are representative of real turbine-APU and generator classes, not vendor-specified for this aircraft.
• Main battery draw is derived from the real motor spec: 8× 6-phase raxial-flux motors (800 hp / 600 kW, 1,250 Nm, 39 kg, 8,500 RPM max, 383mm×135mm each — 6,400 hp / 4,800 kW / 312 kg system total) at ~90% electrical efficiency against the stated 240kWh pack — the ~2.7-minute full-power endurance is a direct consequence of that math, not a separately chosen number. Draw correctly goes to zero whenever the ducted fans are locked/off (Cruise drag-fold, Glide Turbofans-only, or a dead battery) — they aren't spinning, so there's nothing drawing current, which a previous version of this model got wrong.
• The APU (250kW) now has three independent triggers, not just one: online in Hover/Full Electric/Glide (non-Turbofans-only) by default, automatically below 50% battery charge, and in Cruise once the ducted fans drag-lock — since they're drawing zero power at that point anyway, the APU uses the idle capacity to recharge the batteries instead of sitting unused. Combined with the FJ44-4 accessory-box generators (which contribute charge whenever the engines are running, independent of mode), Cruise at high throttle is actually the best charging condition in the whole flight envelope.
• Afterburner speed boost (×1.2247, i.e. √1.5) is derived from a standard 50% afterburning-turbofan thrust increase via v ∝ √thrust for level flight (drag ∝ v²) — a percentage boost applied to base speed, not a flat knot addition, so it scales consistently at any throttle.
• Vertical (Hover) thrust uses the ducted fans' stated 14,500 lbf max combined thrust (all 4 pods, rotated fully vertical at 100% RPM) against the same live gross weight. This caught a real inconsistency: the takeoff throttle was previously fixed at 55%, which only produces ~7,975 lbf — LESS than the ~9,736 lbf full-fuel weight (T/W 0.82, physically incapable of hovering). Takeoff throttle is now 80% (T/W ≈1.19, genuine positive margin). Vertical climb rate is F=ma: excess thrust over weight, divided by mass, capped at 20 ft/sec (1,200 ft/min) — a stricter comfort ceiling than forward flight's, since vertical acceleration is felt more intensely by occupants. The Vertical T/W readout in Engine Health (Ducted Fan Motors) shows this ratio live; it must stay above 1.0 for Hover to be physically possible at all, not just comfortable.
• Climb rate during forward-flight climb uses real thrust-to-weight: twin Williams FJ44-4A engines (3,621 lbf each, real rated thrust) against the aircraft's actual GROSS weight — 4,200 lb dry plus current main (750 gal) and APU (75 gal) fuel on board, converted through real Jet-A density (6.71 lb/gal; full fuel adds ~5,536 lb, for ~9,736 lb gross at 100% fuel). Weight is computed live from remaining fuel, so it drops and climb performance genuinely improves through a flight, same as a real aircraft. Via the excess-thrust-power climb formula (RC = 33,000×(T−W/(L/D))×Vfps/(550W)), T/W ranges from roughly 0.30 (low throttle, full fuel) up to 1.72 (full throttle, fuel exhausted, dry weight only) — still fighter-jet-class at the high end, so the raw physics answer stays enormous, but it's capped at 100 ft/sec (6,000 ft/min) in the model because sustained climb at those rates is limited by occupant comfort and structural loads, not available thrust. The Thrust/Weight readout in Engine Health shows the live, uncapped ratio at current gross weight.

• Autonomous takeoff/climb cycle: selecting a destination with Autonomous Navigation engaged runs the full phase sequence automatically — Hover liftoff (vertical thrust check above) to 300 ft, hand-off to Cruise mode with the forward-thrust climb above, level-off at the profile's chosen cruise altitude, then descent and a final Hover approach at the destination (or the next leg's takeoff, if a refuel stop is involved). None of these transitions require manual mode switching.

Illustrative / not yet physically derived:
• Airspeed-vs-throttle curves (per flight mode) are linear approximations, not lift/drag/thrust-required curves — they don't yet account for the airframe's actual wing loading or the blended-wing-body's lift-to-drag ratio (the climb-rate calculation above assumes L/D=14 as a placeholder pending real wing data).
• Takeoff/transition altitudes and the autopilot's other phase thresholds are reasonable defaults, not performance-chart-derived values.
• GPS/heading integration uses flat-Earth trigonometry (accurate at this scale, <1000nm) rather than great-circle navigation.
• No aerodynamic stall, G-loading, wind, or turbulence model exists anywhere in this simulator.

• Master power and preflight: the START/STOP button is a genuine interlock, not a cosmetic toggle — it refuses to power down while airborne, while Autonomous Nav is engaged, or while the afterburner is active, and every power-on (including the first page load) runs a staggered preflight checklist (battery, turbines, fuel, flight controls, IMU/GPS, ducted fan actuators, body panels, wings, landing-gear and rudder hydraulics, wind, max takeoff weight) with real pass/fail logic tied to actual fuel/battery state, not placeholder text.
• Hover altitude-hold: a real safety fix, not just a feature add. Previously, altitude and throttle were fully independent — nothing stopped an aggressive, unphysical altitude jump. Now, in manual Hover, throttle position relative to the live hover-equilibrium point (computed from current gross weight and the 14,500 lbf max ducted thrust) determines climb/descend direction, and the rate is always capped by the same F=ma-derived vertical climb/descent functions used elsewhere — never instant. This is fully automatic — no target altitude to configure: near equilibrium throttle, it holds the current altitude exactly; away from it, it climbs (capped at the 41,000 ft service ceiling) or descends toward ground. Automated flight phases (takeoff/climb/descent) are unaffected — they already used this same safe-rate logic and are not double-controlled by this addition. Mode switches alone never change altitude, in manual or automated flight.
• Mission-aware afterburner authority: automatic use during Autonomous Nav fires at each 10% milestone of route progress for the current leg (10%, 20%, ... 90%) rather than continuously whenever the cooldown clears — a paced schedule, not an always-on cycle. Efficient profile still never auto-engages (maximum range/economy is the whole point of that profile). A manual ENGAGE A/B press still works anytime above 10,000 ft, independent of this schedule — the milestone system only governs the AI's own automatic decisions. The autopilot still climbs above 10,000 ft to make it legally accessible regardless of profile; whether it's actually fired automatically follows the milestone schedule.
• Sensor-guided landing: destinations are tagged as known airports (the preset dropdown) or arbitrary coordinates (the custom lat/lon field). Landing at a preset airport logs a normal approach; landing at any custom coordinate — a private strip, a field, anywhere without runway/approach data — explicitly logs that the AI is relying on onboard cameras, LIDAR, and the ultrasonic array (already modeled in the Sensor Suite panel) to select a safe touchdown spot, since no known infrastructure exists there.

Explicitly NOT implemented, and why: live Google Maps/radar/flight-tracker integrations, real internet access for the onboard AI, VPN and multi-level encryption, and remote-access intrusion alerts were requested but are not included. This is a static HTML/JS file with no server and no real network stack — building fake UI badges claiming these (e.g., a "VPN: CONNECTED" indicator that does nothing) would misrepresent real capability, which matters more than usual given this feeds a grant proposal. These belong in a separate, honest systems-architecture document if needed, not simulated here.

• Spec reconciliation (build_list.docx / Flight_Calculations.docx): these documents conflict with several already-verified figures in this model. Per instruction, each conflict was resolved in favor of the higher-performing number: service ceiling raised 35,000→41,000 ft, max ducted thrust raised 14,200→14,500 lbf, and the ducted-fan thin-air RPM cap altitude raised 20,000→25,000 ft (all takeoff-throttle/T/W figures elsewhere in this panel were recomputed against the new thrust rating, not left stale). The documents' 8×11kW motor figure (88kW total) and 200kWh battery figure were both LOWER than what's already modeled (8×600kW motors, 240kWh pack) and were therefore kept as-is, not adopted.
• Cruise burn figure — reconciled, not just picked: the documents' flat 23.6 gal/hr combined cruise burn at 41,000 ft conflicted with this model's ~140 lb/hr per-engine idle baseline (a real Williams FJ44-4 figure). Rather than discard one number for the other, the fuel-flow model now includes a genuine altitude correction — real turbofans burn markedly less fuel per throttle % at altitude (thinner air, lower mass flow through the core). The correction scales linearly from 1.0× at sea level to a calibrated 0.2302× at 41,000 ft, chosen so 60% throttle at 41,000 ft lands on exactly 23.6 gal/hr combined, matching the document precisely, while sea-level behavior (the 59% efficiency peak, top-throttle range figures, everything already verified) is completely unchanged, since the correction factor is 1.0× at 0 ft. It's a straight-line approximation rather than a full ISA density model, but it's anchored to a real external number, not invented to look plausible. Applied everywhere fuel burn is computed — Engine Health display, tank drain, and the AI's Auto-profile range-margin check all use the aircraft's actual current altitude now, not a fixed sea-level assumption. Pre-flight route planning (before the aircraft has climbed anywhere) uses a documented 10,000 ft planning assumption rather than ground-level, since a real flight spends most of its distance at cruise altitude, not on the ramp.

What this means for real-world use: the propulsion, fuel, electrical, and now climb-performance subsystems are internally consistent and traceable to real unit conversions and rated engine thrust, making them a reasonable starting point for engineering discussion or a grant technical appendix. Cruise airspeed and drag-side aerodynamics still need real wing loading and L/D data before they should be treated as predictive of actual aircraft performance.
